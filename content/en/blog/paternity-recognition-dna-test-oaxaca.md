Establishing legal **paternity (*reconocimiento de paternidad*) in Oaxaca, Mexico** guarantees fundamental constitutional rights for children, including legal identity, dual surnames, mandatory child support, and statutory inheritance rights.

Whether you are a mother seeking to enforce an absent parent's obligations or a father establishing legal filiation for your child, Mexican law provides a structured judicial pathway through **Family Court Filiation Proceedings (*Juicio Ordinario de Filiación*)**.

---

## 🧬 Forensic DNA Testing: The Gold Standard

In Mexican paternity proceedings, **court-admissible forensic DNA testing** serves as the definitive evidence:

- **Accuracy Exceeding 99.99%:** Granted full evidentiary weight by Oaxaca Family Courts.
- **Chain of Custody Supervision:** Samples are collected directly before court-appointed forensic geneticists to ensure absolute integrity.

### ⚠️ Refusal Consequences: Statutory Presumption of Paternity
If an alleged parent is formally served and **unreasonably refuses to attend the court-ordered DNA test**, the Oaxaca Civil Code and Supreme Court precedent enforce the **statutory presumption of paternity (*presunción legal de paternidad*)**, officially recognizing fatherhood and issuing child support orders.

---

## 💡 Enforceable Rights Derived from a Filiation Decree

Upon securing a favorable final judgment:

1. **Mandatory Child Support:** Immediate establishment of monthly support orders via payroll garnishment or direct deposit.
2. **Retroactive Child Support:** Right to claim backdated child support **from the child's date of birth**.
3. **Official Name & Identity:** Mandatory registration of the father's surname and issuance of a corrected birth certificate by the Oaxaca Civil Registry.
4. **Inheritance Rights:** Automatic status as a first-tier intestate heir under Mexican succession law.

---

## 📝 Required Documents for Filing

To initiate paternity litigation in Oaxaca, petitioners must provide:

- Child's official birth certificate.
- Petitioner's official photo ID (Passport, Resident Card, or INE).
- Respondent's verifiable home or employment address for process service.
- Corroborating evidence (correspondence, witness statements, or photographs if available).
