Filing for **child support in Oaxaca** does not start with a form: it starts with evidence. The route is a claim before the Family Court, supported by documents that prove kinship, the claimant's needs and the payer's ability to pay. When the file is well built, the judge can set **provisional support** within the first working days.

This guide explains the real order of the process, which documents carry weight, and what to do when the obligor has no payroll, lives in another state or simply does not pay.

## Who can file

- **Minor children:** through the person holding parental authority or custody.
- **Children over 18 who study:** if they prove they are still in education and financially dependent.
- **Spouse or common-law partner:** when they devoted themselves to the home or childcare and lack sufficient income.
- **Ascendants:** in specific cases of need and absence of other obligors.

Standing is not presumed: it is proven with a birth certificate, marriage certificate or proof of *concubinato*, together with the economic situation.

## Where to file

The claim is filed before the competent **Family Court**. Jurisdiction is generally determined by the defendant's domicile or the claimant's residence, under the applicable procedural rules.

In Oaxaca de Juárez the family courts are concentrated in the **Ciudad Judicial** (Reyes Mantecón). If the obligor lives in another judicial district, a letter rogatory is requested so that the court at their domicile can serve process and conduct proceedings.

## Documents the file needs

**Kinship and standing**
- The child's birth certificate.
- Marriage certificate or proof of *concubinato*, if applicable.
- Official ID of the person filing.

**The claimant's needs**
- School fees, uniforms and supplies.
- Medical receipts, medication and treatment.
- Rent, utilities, transport and food.
- Documented extraordinary expenses (therapy, equipment, caregiver).

**The payer's ability to pay**
- Pay stubs or an income certificate.
- Bank statements and account activity.
- Property, vehicles, businesses and economic activity.
- Social media and witness evidence when there are no formal documents.

**Settlement proposal**
- Custody, visitation, support and payment method. Presenting it at the start speeds up negotiation and structures the case.

## Step by step

1. **Prepare the file.** Evidence is gathered, a realistic scenario is calculated and strategy is defined (negotiation or litigation).
2. **File the claim.** The claim requests the support order and, in the same filing, **provisional support**.
3. **Admission and provisional support.** The judge admits the claim and can set an immediate amount with the available documentation.
4. **Service on the defendant.** They are summoned to answer, offer evidence and propose a settlement.
5. **Hearing and evidence.** Income, needs and, where relevant, custody and visitation are proven.
6. **Judgment.** Final support, payment method and, if applicable, arrears are set.
7. **Enforcement.** If payment stops: wage withholding, attachment of accounts or assets, and registration in the child-support debtors registry.

## How long it takes

- **Provisional support:** working days after admission.
- **Settlement or agreed judgment:** a few months.
- **Contested case:** several months, depending on evidence, expert reports and the court's caseload.

Timing does not depend only on the judge: a complete file avoids delays caused by formal deficiencies and requests for missing documents.

## What it costs

The cost depends on complexity, on whether assets must be investigated, and on whether the matter settles or goes to judgment. At the first consultation you receive a written budget with scope and stages. The initial consultation is free.

## Special situations

**No payroll.** The standard of living is investigated and support is requested based on the UMA or minimum wage, plus socioeconomic studies.

**Obligor in another state.** A letter rogatory is requested; the case continues in Oaxaca and proceedings are conducted where they live.

**Obligor abroad.** Service is effected through diplomatic or consular channels; assets in Oaxaca can be attached. Coordination with correspondent counsel speeds this up.

**Risk or violence.** Protective measures and, where appropriate, provisional custody can be requested together with support.

## Mistakes that delay support

1. Filing without expense documents.
2. Not requesting provisional support in the same filing.
3. Hiding your own income or the payer's.
4. Not challenging provisional support when it is too low.
5. Waiting months to enforce an existing judgment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can file for child support in Oaxaca?
Minor children, children over 18 who are still studying, a spouse or common-law partner who devoted themselves to the home, and in certain cases ascendants. Standing depends on proving kinship or the relationship and the economic need.

### Where is a child support claim filed?
Before the competent Family Court in Oaxaca, generally at the defendant's domicile or where the claimant lives. In Oaxaca de Juárez the family courts are concentrated in the Ciudad Judicial.

### How long does provisional child support take?
The judge can set it when the claim is admitted, within the first working days, based on the documentation available. You do not have to wait for the final judgment to have a payment order.

### What if the other parent has no formal job?
The obligation continues. Income, property, vehicles and standard of living are investigated, and the court can set support based on the UMA or minimum wage plus socioeconomic studies.

### Can I file if the other parent lives in another state or country?
Yes. If they live in Mexico, a letter rogatory is sent to the court at their domicile. If they live abroad, service is effected through diplomatic or consular channels and enforcement targets assets located in Oaxaca when they exist.
