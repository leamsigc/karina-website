**Wage garnishment for child support** (*embargo de sueldo por pensión alimenticia*) in Oaxaca is the primary enforcement mechanism used by Family Courts to secure immediate and uninterrupted financial support for children and entitled dependents.

Whether enforcing unpaid child support against an uncooperative parent or challenging an excessive wage withholding percentage, understanding the legal mechanics under Mexican law is essential.

---

## ⚖️ How Wage Garnishment Works under Mexican Law

Unlike commercial debt or personal loan disputes (where statutory minimum wages are shielded from seizure), Article 123 of the **Mexican Constitution** and the **Civil Code of the State of Oaxaca** grant child support absolute priority over all other financial liabilities:

1. **Court Garnishment Order:** The Family Court judge issues an official writ (*oficio de retención*) directly to the employer's HR or payroll department.
2. **Mandatory Source Deduction:** The employer is legally mandated to deduct the court-ordered percentage **at the payroll source** before disbursing net earnings.
3. **Direct Transfer:** Withheld funds are wired directly to the custodial parent's bank account or deposited into the Family Court escrow account.

---

## 💰 Garnishable Compensation (Complete Checklist)

Wage garnishment writs in Oaxaca cover total financial remuneration, including:

- **Base salary, hourly pay, and overtime.**
- **Mandatory Christmas bonuses (*Aguinaldo*) and vacation premiums.**
- **Productivity, attendance, and quarterly incentives.**
- **Profit-sharing distributions (*PTU*).**
- **Severance packages, lay-off settlements, and retirement payouts.**

### ⚠️ AFORE Retirement Fund Seizure During Unemployment
Under landmark rulings by the Supreme Court of Mexico (SCJN), if an obligor is unemployed and lacks other seizure-eligible assets, Family Court judges can order the **direct seizure of accumulated retirement funds (AFORE subaccounts)** to satisfy child support arrears.

---

## 🚫 Employer Penalties for Non-Compliance

Employers who ignore judicial garnishment writs or misreport worker earnings face severe legal liabilities:
- **Joint Financial Liability:** Employers become personally liable for unpaid child support debts.
- **Judicial Fines & Arrest:** Direct contempt sanctions enforced by the Family Court.
- **Criminal Charges:** Disobedience of judicial mandates under the Oaxaca State Penal Code.

---

## 📊 Formal vs. Informal Income Enforcement Matrix

| Feature | Formal W-2 / Payroll Employee | Independent Contractor / Informal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Execution Time** | Immediate upon HR service of notice. | Requires asset tracing and public registry searches. |
