The international departure of minors from Oaxaca abroad requires strict compliance with Mexican immigration regulations and international treaties such as the **Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction**.

If you plan to travel, vacation, or relocate outside Mexico with your children and require legal travel authorization or face cross-border custody disputes, this guide outlines the current legal rules.

---

## ✈️ Minor Travel Permits Leaving Mexico (SAM Form & INAMI)

Under Mexican Immigration Law (*Ley de Migración*), minor travel abroad requires strict formalities:

1. **Notarial / Consular Authorization:** If traveling alone or with a third party, both parents must sign formal consent before a Notary Public or Mexican Consulate.
2. **SAM Form (*Formato SAM*):** Free official authorization validated by the National Institute of Migration (INAMI) at international departure points.
3. **Court-Ordered Travel Authorization (*Venia Judicial*):** If one parent unreasonably refuses to sign passport applications or travel permissions, the Oaxaca Family Court Judge can issue a **judicial substitute authorization**.

---

## 🌐 The Hague Convention & International Child Abduction

If a parent removes or retains a minor in another country without the legal consent of the other parent or a competent judge:

- Urgent **international child return proceedings** are activated.
- Direct intervention by the Mexican Central Authority (Ministry of Foreign Affairs - SRE).
- Summary return trial conducted in Oaxaca Family Courts to order the minor's immediate return to their country of habitual residence.

---

## 📝 Requirements for Judicial Travel Permits in Oaxaca

For a Family Court Judge to grant travel authorization without the other parent's signature:

- Proof of educational, cultural, or vacation benefits for the child.
- Submission of round-trip airline tickets and detailed accommodation itineraries.
- Guarantees ensuring the child's timely return to Mexican territory.
