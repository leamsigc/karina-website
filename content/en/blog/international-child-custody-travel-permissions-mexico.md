International relocation, cross-border parenting, or traveling abroad with minor children from Oaxaca, Mexico requires strict compliance with Mexican Immigration Law and international treaties such as the **Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction**.

Whether securing mandatory travel consent, resolving international custody disputes, or preventing unlawful parental removal, understanding the statutory requirements under Mexican law is essential.

---

## ✈️ Mandatory Minor Travel Permissions in Mexico (SAM & INAMI)

Under Mexican Immigration Law (*Ley de Migración*), minors under 18 leaving Mexican territory without both parents must present official documentation at border controls:

1. **Notarial or Consular Authorization:** Certified consent form signed by both parents holding parental rights (*patria potestad*).
2. **INAMI SAM Form (*Formato de Salida de Menores*):** Mandatory official authorization registered with Mexican Immigration officers at airports.
3. **Judicial Travel Authorization (*Venia Judicial*):** If one parent unreasonably refuses consent for passport issuance or international travel, an Oaxaca Family Court Judge can issue an **overriding judicial travel authorization**.

---

## 🌐 Hague Convention & Cross-Border Parental Abduction

If a parent wrongfully removes or retains a child in another country without legal authorization or court approval:

- The non-custodial parent can initiate expedited **Hague Abduction Convention Return Proceedings**.
- Coordination between Central Authorities (Ministry of Foreign Affairs - SRE in Mexico, US State Department, etc.).
- Oaxaca Family Courts issue immediate location orders and hearing mandates for prompt child return to their habitual residence.

---

## 📝 Securing Judicial Travel Authorization in Oaxaca

To obtain court authorization when a parent refuses to sign:

- Demonstrate clear educational, medical, or recreational benefits for the child.
- Provide roundtrip flight itineraries, proof of lodging, and return guarantees.
- Post judicial bond (*fianza*) to ensure timely return to Mexican jurisdiction.
