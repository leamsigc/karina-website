**Paternity recognition** is a non-waivable human right guaranteed under the Mexican Constitution and the Oaxaca Civil Code. Every child holds an inalienable right to **identity, legal surnames, biological origin, and financial support**.

Both mothers seeking to enforce parental responsibility against an absent father and fathers asserting denied paternity rights can initiate an **Ordinary Filiation Lawsuit before Family Courts**.

---

## 🧬 Expert DNA Genetic Testing: Conclusive Court Evidence

In paternity recognition trials in Oaxaca, the definitive piece of evidence is **expert DNA genetic testing**:

- **Over 99.99% Accuracy:** Fully admitted as conclusive evidence by the Superior Court of Justice of Oaxaca.
- **Supervised Sample Collection:** Conducted before a court-certified genetic expert designated by the judge to ensure chain of custody.

### ⚠️ Consequence of Refusal: Legal Presumption of Paternity
If the alleged father is lawfully served and **unjustifiably refuses to submit to DNA sample collection**, the Civil Code and Supreme Court precedent mandate the judge to declare a **legal presumption of paternity**, officially establishing filiation and ordering child support payments.

---

## 💡 Immediate Legal Rights Established by Court Judgment

Upon securing a favorable final judgment in a filiation lawsuit:

1. **Ongoing Child Support:** Immediate establishment of payroll garnishment or monthly child support.
2. **Retroactive Child Support:** Claiming all past healthcare, education, and living expenses unpaid **since the child's birth date**.
3. **Legal Surnames & Identity:** Formal assignment of the father's surname and correction of the birth certificate at the Oaxaca Civil Registry.
4. **Inheritance Rights:** Full statutory heir status in estate probate proceedings.

---

## 📝 Filing Requirements & Documentation

To initiate a paternity lawsuit in Oaxaca, we require:

- Child's current birth certificate.
- Petitioner's official photo ID (Passport / Voter ID).
- Exact address for serving process on the alleged father.
- Supporting evidence (text messages, photos, proof of relationship).
