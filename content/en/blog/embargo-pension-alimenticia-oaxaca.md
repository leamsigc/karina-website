**Wage garnishment for child support in Oaxaca** is the most effective legal tool for forced enforcement, ensuring obligors fulfill their ongoing child support duties immediately and without interruption.

Whether you are seeking to enforce delinquent child support payments or facing excessive payroll deductions, this guide explains the step-by-step procedure before **Family Courts in Oaxaca**.

---

## ⚖️ How Court Garnishment Orders Work in Oaxaca

Unlike commercial or bank debt (where minimum wages are immune from garnishment), Article 123 of the Mexican Constitution and the Oaxaca Civil Code establish that **child support holds absolute legal priority**:

1. **Issuance of the Deduction Order:** The Family Judge issues and serves an official judicial order directly to the employer's HR or payroll department.
2. **Withholding at the Source:** The employer is legally obligated to deduct the specified percentage or fixed amount **before releasing payroll** to the employee.
3. **Direct Deposit:** Withheld funds are transferred directly to the custodial parent's bank account or deposited into the Oaxaca State Court escrow account.

---

## 💰 Garnishable Compensation & Earnings (Complete Checklist)

Judicial child support garnishment orders encompass total ordinary and extraordinary employee earnings:

- **Base salary and overtime pay.**
- **Annual Christmas bonus (*Aguinaldo*) and vacation premium.**
- **Punctuality, attendance, and performance bonuses.**
- **Commissions and mandatory profit-sharing (*PTU*).**
- **Severance packages and termination payouts.**

### ⚠️ AFORE Retirement Fund Garnishment in Unemployment Cases
Under recent Mexican Supreme Court rulings, if an obligor lacks formal employment and has no attached real estate or vehicle assets, Family Court judges in Oaxaca are authorized to order **forced garnishment of AFORE retirement subaccount funds** to satisfy child support arrears.

---

## 🚫 Employer Sanctions for Non-Compliance

Employers failing to execute judicial garnishment orders or concealing real wages face severe legal penalties:
- **Joint Liability (*Responsabilidad Solidaria*):** The employer becomes personally liable to pay unwithheld support out of corporate funds.
- **Fines & Administrative Arrest:** Enforcement measures imposed directly by the Family Judge.
- **Criminal Penalties:** Criminal charges under the Oaxaca Penal Code for contempt of judicial orders.

---

## 📊 Formal vs. Informal Obligor Comparison

| Legal Aspect | Formal Employee Obligor | Informal / Self-Employed Obligor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Collection Method** | Direct institutional payroll deduction order. | Attachment of bank accounts, real estate, or vehicles. |
| **Deduction Amount** | Direct percentage of net earnings (15% - 50%). | Fixed monthly fee set in UMA / Minimum Wage units. |
| **Enforcement Speed** | Immediate upon employer service of process. | Requires financial investigations and registry searches. |

---

## 📝 Legal Action Plan: Next Steps for Your Case

### For Custodial Parents (Recipients):
1. File initial child support claims or post-judgment enforcement motions.
2. Request immediate payroll garnishment orders and register delinquent obligors in the **Delinquent Obligor Registry (*Ley Sabina*)**.
3. File criminal non-support charges in cases of persistent non-compliance.

### For Paying Parents (Obligors):
1. If garnishment percentages are excessive or income has decreased, we file an **incident for child support reduction**.
2. Prevent illegal garnishments exceeding statutory limits.
