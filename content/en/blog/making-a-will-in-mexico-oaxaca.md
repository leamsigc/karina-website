If you own real estate, bank accounts, business shares, or coastal bank trusts (*fideicomisos*) in **Oaxaca, Puerto Escondido, or Huatulco**, executing a **Mexican Public Open Will (*Testamento Público Abierto*)** before a Mexican Public Notary is the single most critical asset protection measure you can take.

Executing a Mexican Will prevents your heirs from undergoing a costly, multi-year **Intestate Succession Trial (*Juicio Intestamentario*)** in Oaxaca courts.

---

## 🏛️ Why Your US or Canadian Will May Fail in Mexico

While foreign wills can theoretically be recognized in Mexico:

- They require a lengthy court validation process (*Exequátur*).
- They require **international Apostilles and certified court translations** into Spanish.
- A **Mexican Will** drafted specifically for your Mexican assets is registered directly with the **National Registry of Wills (*RNAT*)**, enabling fast, out-of-court notary probate execution upon death.

---

## 📜 Structuring Your Mexican Will

Under Mexican Civil Law, a Public Open Will allows tailored asset distributions:

1. **Universal Heirs (*Herederos*):** Beneficiaries designated to inherit percentages of the total estate.
2. **Specific Legatees (*Legatarios*):** Beneficiaries designated to receive specific assets (e.g., a specific home or bank account in Oaxaca).
3. **Executor (*Albacea*):** The trusted person or legal counselor appointed to manage estate distribution.

---

## 📝 Requirements for Expats Executing a Will in Oaxaca

- **Valid Passport:** Official photo identification.
- **Residency Card / Visa Status:** If applicable.
- **Property Documentation:** Copies of property deeds (*escrituras*) or bank trust (*fideicomiso*) agreements.
- **Sworn Official Interpreter:** Mandated if the testator does not speak fluent Spanish.
