## When commercial collection makes sense

Commercial collection applies when there is an obligation of a commercial nature: a signed promissory note, an acknowledged invoice, a bounced cheque, a supply contract or a debt acknowledgement. The goal is to recover principal, interest and, where applicable, collection costs.

It is time to act when:

- The due date passed and the debtor stopped responding.
- Promised payments never arrived.
- The debtor is still operating but avoids contact.
- Assets or accounts exist that can be secured.
- The instrument is close to its limitation period and the executive route must be preserved.

## Out-of-court or court collection

**Out-of-court collection.** A formal demand letter with a deadline is sent, the response is documented and a payment agreement is negotiated. It is faster and cheaper when the debtor intends to pay or fears litigation.

**Court collection.** If payment does not follow, a commercial executive lawsuit is filed (when the instrument carries execution) or an ordinary lawsuit. This allows a precautionary attachment, account intervention and, ultimately, auction of assets.

The choice depends on the instrument, the amount, the debtor's solvency and available time. A well-drafted promissory note opens the executive route; a commercial relationship without an instrument usually requires an ordinary action with more evidence.

## Documents that strengthen the file

- **Promissory note or cheque** in original form, with endorsements and complete details.
- **Invoice** with acknowledgement of receipt and purchase order.
- **Signed contract** and annexes.
- **Delivery receipts** for goods or services.
- **Emails, messages and statements** where the debt is acknowledged.
- **Partial payments** that confirm or interrupt the obligation.

The quality of the instrument defines the strategy. An incomplete document does not necessarily prevent collection, but it changes the route and the timeline.

## How we handle the case

1. **Instrument review.** Signature, due date, endorsements, limitation periods and the debtor's solvency.
2. **Demand letter.** Formal notice with a deadline and warning, with proof of delivery.
3. **Negotiation.** Payment agreement, security or debt acknowledgement when it benefits the client.
4. **Filing.** Commercial executive or ordinary lawsuit before the competent court.
5. **Precautionary measures.** Attachment of accounts, assets or income to secure the outcome.
6. **Judgment and enforcement.** Payment is ordered and, if ignored, the auction stage follows.

## How long it takes and what it costs

An uncontested case can resolve in a few months; with exceptions and appeals it takes longer. Effective recovery depends on the existence of locatable assets.

Fees are quoted in writing according to the amount, the route and the complexity. At the first consultation the instrument is reviewed free of charge and we explain whether negotiation or litigation is preferable.

## Mistakes that complicate collection

1. **Waiting too long.** Limitation periods and disappearing assets work against you.
2. **Losing the original** promissory note or cheque.
3. **Not documenting delivery** of the goods or service.
4. **Accepting payments without a receipt** or written agreement.
5. **Suing without investigating** whether the debtor has assets.

## Frequently asked questions

### What documents do I need to collect a commercial debt?
The instrument that proves the obligation: promissory note, cheque, invoice with acknowledgement, signed contract or debt acknowledgement. Delivery receipts, emails and any communication where the debtor admits the debt strengthen the file.

### How long does a commercial executive lawsuit take in Oaxaca?
An uncontested case can resolve in a few months. If the debtor opposes, files exceptions or appeals, it takes longer. Attachment and auction depend on whether locatable assets exist.

### Can I collect if the debtor has no assets?
A judgment can still be obtained, but effective collection requires assets. That is why accounts, property and business activity are investigated before or during the case, and precautionary measures are considered.

### Does the right to collect a promissory note expire?
Yes. The bill-of-exchange action has limitation periods that run from the due date. The instrument should be reviewed and acted on promptly so the executive route is not lost.

### Can I collect an invoice without a signed contract?
Yes, if the commercial relationship is proven: invoice, purchase order, delivery acknowledgement, emails and partial payments. The quality of the documents determines whether the executive or ordinary route applies.
